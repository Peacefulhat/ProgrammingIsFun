- Mathematically a **vector** is an element of a vector space(a structure that displays certain mathematical properties for additions and multiplications).
- **Vectors** in physics, a quantity that has both magnitude and direction.
- A vector space (or linear space) is a fundamental concept in linear algebra and mathematics. It is a set of objects called vectors, which can be added together and multiplied by scalars (numbers) while satisfying certain properties. 
 
- **Vectors are usually represented as an ordered list of numbers that can be treated in a similar way to a single number in an algebraic equation.**
 
## `2D` Vectors

A `2D` vector **v** is represented as:

$$
\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}
$$


Alternatively, it can also be written as:

$$
\mathbf{v} = x \hat{i} + y \hat{j}
$$

where:
- **x** and **y** are the components along the x-axis and y-axis, respectively.
- `î` and `ĵ` are the unit vectors in the x and y directions.

### Magnitude of a `2D` Vector
The magnitude (or length) of a `2D` vector is given by:

$$
|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}
$$

### Direction of a `2D` Vector
The direction (angle **θ** with the positive x-axis) is:

$$
\theta = \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{y}{x} \right)
$$

## `3D` Vectors

- A `3D` vector **v** is represented as:

$$
\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix}
$$

Alternatively, it can be written as:

$$
\mathbf{v} = x \hat{i} + y \hat{j} + z \hat{k}
$$

where:
- **x**, **y**, and **z** are the components along the x, y, and z axes, respectively.
- `î`, `ĵ`, and **k̂** are the unit vectors in the x, y, and z directions.

### Magnitude of a `3D` Vector
The magnitude of a `3D` vector is:

$$
|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}
$$

### Direction Cosines
The angles **α, β, γ** that the vector makes with the x, y, and z axes are given by:

$$math
\cos \alpha = \frac{x}{|\mathbf{v}|}, \quad \cos \beta = \frac{y}{|\mathbf{v}|}, \quad \cos \gamma = \frac{z}{|\mathbf{v}|}
$$
